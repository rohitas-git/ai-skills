# E-commerce blog writing playbook

## Topic and intent

Start from a decision the shopper needs to make. Use supplied support themes, product questions, returns
reasons, objections, store search, or current search research. Preserve customer privacy and distinguish
observed questions from proposed angles. A high-intent topic is relevant to a concrete need; it need not
contain “buy” or “best.” Do not manufacture keyword volume, difficulty, or conversion forecasts.

For example, “Which carry-on backpack fits a weekend trip?” provides a clearer job than “Travel essentials.”
Check whether the shop can answer the question credibly and offer a useful next step before choosing it.

| Format | Reader's job | Useful structure |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer's guide | Choose the right option | needs and constraints → selection criteria → options → trade-offs → next step |
| Tutorial | Complete a task | expected result → prerequisites → ordered steps → troubleshooting → relevant supplies |
| Lifestyle/inspiration | Picture a useful possibility | concrete scenario → ideas in context → adaptations → products that enable them |
| Comparison | Decide between alternatives | who each suits → common criteria → evidence → drawbacks → conditional recommendation |

## Evidence and honest recommendations

Use exact product pages, manufacturer manuals, supplied test evidence, and reliable sources for factual
statements. Check current prices and availability if included; specify currency, market, and date as needed.
Do not invent URLs, discounts, stock urgency, certifications, reviews, or endorsements. A manufacturer's
description can support a specification, but it is not independent proof of performance.

Compare the same versions and units on the same criteria. Keep unknown values unknown. Include who should
choose each option, who may prefer an alternative, and meaningful drawbacks. Explain the merchant's commercial
interest. For affiliate/sponsored content, disclose the relationship clearly where readers encounter it and
follow the applicable market's current rules.

Claims such as “best,” “tested,” and “our favorite” need a stated basis. Identify actual test methods, sample,
conditions, and limitations when supplied. Never imply hands-on use from desk research. Without adequate testing,
write an explicitly research-based selection guide or propose evaluation criteria instead of a fabricated ranking.

## Headline and search presentation

Make the promise specific, useful, and accurate. Include the main topic naturally, near the beginning when it
reads well. Avoid clickbait, repetitive keywords, and dates added merely to imply freshness.

A roughly 55–60 character meta title can be a drafting convenience, not a guaranteed display limit. Google
truncates to fit display width and may generate a different title link. The on-page H1 may differ where useful,
but both must describe the same article. Meta descriptions should summarize real value, not promise guaranteed
snippets. Suggest a short descriptive slug and verify existing URLs before proposing a change to a live page.

There is no SEO requirement for 1,000–2,500 words. Use that as an optional brief range for a substantial guide,
not evidence that longer copy performs better. Answer the question fully without padding. Use natural related
language rather than exact-match repetition or keyword-density targets.

## Storytelling and readability

Open with a recognizable problem or goal, a clear benefit, and enough immediate information to reward reading.
Use a concrete scenario or example to show the product's role. Label invented scenarios as illustrative; never
pass them off as customer stories. Keep narrative relevant to the shopper's task.

Use short paragraphs, usually a few sentences each. “Three to four lines” is a visual heuristic because line
length varies by device and font. Use clear H2/H3 headings, lists for parallel points or steps, and tables for
comparable choices. Bold sparingly; use blockquotes only for genuine, attributed quotations. Avoid decorative
formatting, generic openings, repeated conclusions, and exaggerated promises.

Match the store's vocabulary, tone, and visual direction. Recommend product photography, demonstrations, or
short videos when they clarify the task. Describe needed assets and their placement; check rights and product
accuracy before reuse. Alt text should explain relevant visible content, not repeat target keywords.

## Natural product placement

Connect **reader need → relevant feature → practical benefit → product link**. For instance, explain the packing
constraint before linking a bag with verified dimensions. Demonstrate a method before recommending the tool
that makes a step easier. Avoid inserting a product into every section or suppressing a simpler solution.

Use descriptive anchors that make the destination clear. Include complementary products only when useful to
the task. Preserve the article's usefulness even if the reader does not buy from this store.

## CTA by reader readiness

| Reader state | Possible next action |
|---|---|
| Exploring a problem | Read a related guide or subscribe for relevant advice |
| Learning a method | View instructions, find the right supplies, or save a checklist |
| Comparing choices | Compare verified specifications or visit the relevant collection |
| Ready to select | View the specific product or choose the verified size/model |

These are examples, not mandatory funnel rules. Use the action the article has prepared the reader to take.
Choose one primary CTA, with secondary links only where useful. Explain the value of the next step. Do not
promise an “Add to Cart” action unless the destination supports it; a product-page link is often more suitable.

## Final editorial pass

Confirm that the article answers the reader's question and fulfills its headline; the examples are concrete;
every product mention has a clear role; factual claims and links are supported; comparisons are fair; the voice
matches the store; and the primary CTA fits the reader's readiness. Note missing product facts and assets.
For publication-ready requests, make the remaining checks explicit without inventing completion or results.

## Primary sources

Checked 2026-10-24. Recheck current search guidance when making platform-specific recommendations.

- [Google: People-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content): originality, useful answers, accurate titles, and no preferred word count.
- [Google: Title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link): descriptive concise titles, no fixed character limit, and display-width truncation.
- [Google: High-quality reviews](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews): decision criteria, benefits and drawbacks, original evidence, and support for best-of recommendations.
- [Google: SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide): reader-focused language, descriptive links, images, and metadata.
