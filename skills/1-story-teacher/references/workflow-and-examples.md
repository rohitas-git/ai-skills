# Optional narrative-design guide

This guide supplies prompts, not mandatory phases or output sizes. The active contract is
the parent `SKILL.md`.

## Fidelity-first design

For the one selected learning object, distinguish:

| Ledger item | Question |
|---|---|
| Source-backed idea | What does the supplied material actually support? |
| Caveat | What uncertainty or exception must survive compression? |
| Simplification | What detail is omitted for this audience or length? |
| Fictional invention | What exists only to make the narrative work? |

Use the ledger internally unless the user requests a mapping. A story consequence can
illustrate an idea, but it is not evidence that the idea is universally true.

## Possible structures

Choose one only when it fits:

- a character predicts or solves a problem using the idea;
- two approaches produce contrasting consequences;
- a mystery reveals a hidden relationship;
- a speculative rule makes an abstract relationship tangible;
- an ordinary workplace or domestic situation provides a low-drama analogy.

High stakes, fantasy, heists, twists, and failure are options—not defaults. Avoid forcing
technical vocabulary into dialogue or making every concept a separate scene.

## Self-check

- Does the story answer the requested learning purpose?
- Could a reader distinguish factual source content from invented story mechanics?
- Did compression reverse causality, erase a material caveat, or create certainty?
- Are the prose, characters, setting, and plot original?
- Did the requested audience, length, tone, and exclusions survive?

For staged long-form work, stop at the exact outline/chapter authorized and return the
continuation point. Do not manufacture a chapter count or promise automatic continuation.
