# Natural voice pass

Use this when the requested marketing copy edit is about stiffness or recognizable generated prose. The goal is clear, credible copy in the author's voice; there is no reliable test that proves who wrote text.

1. If the author supplies a writing sample, note its sentence rhythm, vocabulary, formality, perspective, and transitions. Match those traits without copying distinctive passages.
2. Mark actual problems in the supplied copy: inflated importance, generic promotion, vague attribution, needless scene-setting, repeated three-part lists, synonym cycling, filler, over-hedging, and repeated announcement phrases. Treat these as prompts for judgment, not forbidden-word rules.
3. Replace vague claims with specific supplied facts or label them for verification. Never invent a study, quote, person, experience, feeling, or first-person anecdote to make copy seem authentic.
4. Vary sentence shape where it improves reading. Keep intentional repetition, technical terms, punctuation, and brand voice when they help. Preserve meaning, claim strength, evidence, legal qualifiers, and the user's requested tone.
5. Read the result once for residual formulaic wording and factual drift. Deliver the edited copy plus a short explanation of meaningful changes when useful; avoid mandatory multi-draft rituals.
